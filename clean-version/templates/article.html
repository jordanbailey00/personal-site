I. Opening

Begin with a question worth spending time on.

Every essay begins with a little room to think. This is a preview of the reading layout: a place for an opening question, a careful explanation, and the details that make an idea useful.1

The main argument stays in a comfortable reading column. Supporting footnotes sit beside the passage on wider screens and below it on smaller screens. The main text remains centered in both Classic and Fantasy.

1 A margin note can hold a source, a qualification, or a useful detour without interrupting the thought.

II. An idea, made precise

Move from intuition to a definition.

Use a definition when a term needs a precise meaning. The muted color and small title bar keep it distinct from the surrounding prose.

Definition · A useful idea

The definition goes here. Give the reader the minimum they need to follow the argument, then show what the definition lets them do.

A worked example

A short example can follow a definition. Equations, lists, and tables use the same measure as the text, with room to scroll when a calculation needs more space.
